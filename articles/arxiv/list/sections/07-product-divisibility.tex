\section{Product Divisibility Properties}
\label{sec:product-divisibility}

Every element of a list divides its total product. The proofs below apply
the quotient-invariance-under-shift law
$\operatorname{mod}(a + m \cdot b, b) = \operatorname{mod}(a, b)$ at
$a = 0$ to derive $(a \cdot b) \bmod a = 0$; that law is verified in the
companion article
Division and Modulo from Recursive Normalization \cite{mata2026modulo}
and reused here as a foundational primitive.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \href{https://github.com/thiagomata/prime-numbers/blob/list-article-v1.0.0/src/main/scala/v1/chapter3/list/properties/ListProductDiv.scala}{Head divides product}:
    $\operatorname{product}(L) \bmod \operatorname{head}(L) = 0$
  \item \href{https://github.com/thiagomata/prime-numbers/blob/list-article-v1.0.0/src/main/scala/v1/chapter3/list/properties/ListProductDiv.scala}{All elements divide product}:
    every element divides $\operatorname{product}(L)$
  \item \href{https://github.com/thiagomata/prime-numbers/blob/list-article-v1.0.0/src/main/scala/v1/chapter3/list/properties/ListProductDiv.scala}{Inserted element divides product}:
    $x$ divides $\operatorname{product}(x :: L)$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Head Divides Product}

The head of a positive list divides the product of the entire list.

\begin{gather*}
\forall\, elements \in \mathbb{L},\ elements \neq L_e \\
(\forall\, x \in elements,\ x > 0) \implies \operatorname{product}(elements) \bmod \operatorname{head}(elements) = 0
\end{gather*}

\textbf{Proof:}

\begin{gather*}
\operatorname{product}(elements) = \operatorname{head}(elements) \cdot \operatorname{product}(\operatorname{tail}(elements))
\quad \text{[by definition of product]} \\
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{product}(elements) \bmod \operatorname{head}(elements) \\
&= (\operatorname{head}(elements) \cdot \operatorname{product}(\operatorname{tail}(elements))) \bmod \operatorname{head}(elements) \\
&= 0 \quad \blacksquare \quad \text{[by modulo identity]}
\end{aligned}
\end{gather*}

The full Scala verification code is in Appendix~A.16.

\subsection{All Elements Divide Product}

Every element of a positive list divides the product of the list.

\begin{gather*}
\forall\, elements \in \mathbb{L} \\
(\forall\, x \in elements,\ x > 0) \implies (\forall\, x \in elements,\ \operatorname{product}(elements) \bmod x = 0)
\end{gather*}

\textbf{Proof by induction on $elements$}\par\nopagebreak

\textbf{Base case}: $elements = L_e$ --- vacuously true.\par\nopagebreak

\textbf{Inductive step}: For $\operatorname{head}(p) :: tail$, we have
$\operatorname{product}(elements) = p \cdot \operatorname{product}(tail)$.
By modulo identity, $p$ divides the product. By the inductive hypothesis,
every element of $tail$ appears as the head of some recursive sublist and
divides that sublist's product. Multiplying that sublist product by the
preceding positive factors preserves divisibility, so every tail element
also divides $p \cdot \operatorname{product}(tail) = \operatorname{product}(elements)$.

\subsection{Inserted Element Divides Product}

Inserting an element into a list guarantees that the resulting product is
divisible by that element.

\begin{gather*}
\forall\, prefix, suffix \in \mathbb{L},\ \forall\, e \in \mathbb{S},\ e > 0 \\
(\forall\, x \in prefix,\ x > 0),\ (\forall\, x \in suffix,\ x > 0) \\
\implies \operatorname{product}(prefix \mathbin{\texttt{++}} (e :: suffix)) \bmod e = 0
\end{gather*}

\textbf{Proof:}

\begin{gather*}
\operatorname{product}(prefix \mathbin{\texttt{++}} (e :: suffix)) = e \cdot \operatorname{product}(prefix \mathbin{\texttt{++}} suffix)
\quad \text{[Product Pull-Out Element]} \\
= e \cdot k
\quad \text{[where $k = \operatorname{product}(prefix \mathbin{\texttt{++}} suffix)$]} \\
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{product}(prefix \mathbin{\texttt{++}} (e :: suffix)) \bmod e \\
&= (e \cdot k) \bmod e = 0 \quad \blacksquare \quad \text{[Q.E.D.]}
\end{aligned}
\end{gather*}
