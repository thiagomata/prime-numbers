## Abstract

This article presents a formal verification approach to the Twin Prime Conjecture by analyzing the behavior of "2-gaps" in Sieve Sequences. We hypothesize that the existence of infinite twin prime candidates is a structural invariant of the SieveSequence. The refinement process (filtration by primes) is algebraically incapable of extinguishing all "2-gaps" once the sequence reaches a critical threshold of density. This transforms the problem from an unsolved number theory riddle into a formal verification of a state machine invariant.

## Introduction

The Twin Prime Conjecture states that there are infinitely many prime pairs $(p, p+2)$. While this remains unproven in number theory, we can analyze the behavior of twin prime candidates in the Sieve of Eratosthenes framework.

In the Sieve Sequence framework:
- **"2-gap"**: A gap of exactly 2 units between consecutive elements in the sequence, representing a twin prime candidate
- **Structural Invariant**: A property that persists through all transformations

## Core Concepts

### Key Definitions

* **SieveSequence**: An infinite sequence generated by wheel factorization, represented by a `head` and a `cycle` (MemCycle of gaps).
* **Refinement**: The `next()` operation that filters the sequence by a new prime.
* **"2-gap"**: A gap of size 2 in the cycle, corresponding to potential twin primes.

### Core Assumptions

1. **Uniformity**: Residues coprime to the modulus $M$ are distributed uniformly across residue classes of the new prime $p$.
2. **Filtration Rate**: Each refinement level removes exactly $1/p$ of total elements.
3. **Coupled Destruction**: Removing a residue $r \equiv 0 \pmod p$ destroys/merges the two gaps adjacent to it.

## The Strategy: Capacity-Destruction Constraint

Instead of relying on statistical probability, we rely on a formal verification of a lower bound inequality to prove the survival of twin prime candidates.

### The Survival Inequality

A "2-gap" cannot be fully extinguished if the count of existing 2-gaps ($T_k$) exceeds the maximum destruction potential of the filter ($2 \cdot D_p$).

$$T_k > \frac{2 \cdot |R_k|}{p}$$

Where:
- $T_k$: Number of "2" gaps at level $k$.
- $|R_k|$: Total elements (residues) in the sequence.
- $2 \cdot D_p$: Maximum destruction potential of the filter (since each removed residue affects 2 adjacent gaps).

### Alternative Formulation

The inequality can also be expressed as:
$$T_k > \left\lceil \frac{p}{p-2} \right\rceil$$

This threshold represents the minimum number of 2-gaps required to ensure survival through the next refinement.

## Mathematical Framework

### Refinement Properties

When filtering by prime $p$:
1. The sequence removes approximately $1/p$ of elements
2. Each removed residue affects exactly 2 gaps (the gaps on either side)
3. The total number of gaps in the cycle equals $|R_k| - 1$

### Inductive Invariant

If the inequality $T_k > \frac{2 \cdot |R_k|}{p}$ holds for level $k$, then the structural properties of the `next()` operation ensure it holds for $k+1$.

This is because:
- The destruction potential is bounded by $2 \cdot |R_k| / p$
- If we start with more than this bound in 2-gaps, some must survive
- New 2-gaps can be created from the merging of smaller gaps

## The Safe Regime

### Empirical Observation

Empirical data indicates that for $p \ge 7$, the surplus of 2-gaps diverges - meaning the number of 2-gaps grows faster than the destruction rate.

### Mathematical Consequence

Once the system enters this "Safe Regime", the persistence of twin prime candidates is guaranteed by the structural properties of the transformation, not by statistical likelihood.

## Validation Approach

### Formal Verification with Stainless

We can verify the survival invariant using the Stainless verification system:

1. **Inductive Proof**: Show that if $T_k > \frac{2 \cdot |R_k|}{p_k}$ holds at level $k$, it holds at level $k+1$
2. **Threshold Verification**: Verify that $T_k > \lceil \frac{p_k}{p_k - 2} \rceil$ for all refinements
3. **Diagnostic Monitoring**: Track the ratio $T_k / |R_k|$ to confirm permanent entry into the Safe Regime

### Implementation

The implementation tracks:
- $T_k$: Number of 2-gaps (via `countTwoGaps()`)
- $|R_k|$: Total cycle size (via `cycle.size`)
- Current prime $p_k$: The head value

## Conclusion

This approach shifts the paradigm of the Twin Prime problem:
- From: An unsolved number theory conjecture
- To: A formal verification problem of a state machine invariant

If the survival condition is met at the base level and preserved by the transformation function (`next()`), the existence of twin prime candidates is a mathematical certainty.
