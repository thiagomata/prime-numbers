## Architectural Summary: Sieve Sequence Gap Persistence

This framework formalizes the use of wheel factorization to generate prime candidates while tracking the structural survival of "2" gaps (twin prime candidates).

---

### Core Concepts

* **SieveSequence**: An infinite, deterministic sequence of integers coprime to a modulus $M$, represented by a `head` and a `MemCycle` of gaps.
* **Refinement (Merge)**: The `nextLevel` operation, where the sequence is filtered by a new prime $p$.
* **Gap Persistence**: The hypothesis that "2" gaps (difference between adjacent coprime integers) remain in the sequence indefinitely despite the reduction in residue density.
* **Merge Cost**: The deterministic loss of gaps occurring when a residue $r \equiv 0 \pmod p$ is removed, causing adjacent gaps ($g_1, g_2$) to merge into $g_{new} = g_1 + g_2$.

### Assumptions

* **Uniformity**: Residues coprime to the modulus $M$ are distributed uniformly across residue classes of the new prime $p$.
* **Filtration Rate**: Every refinement removes exactly $1/p$ of the current elements in the `SieveSequence`.
* **Gap Coupling**: The removal of a residue $r$ destroys the two gaps adjacent to it.

### Hypothesis

The existence of "2" gaps is a **structural invariant** of the Sieve Sequence. While the density of "2" gaps decreases at a rate proportional to $1 - 2/p$, the total number of "2" gaps $T_k$ remains strictly positive and eventually grows (in absolute count) as the total sequence length increases, ensuring twin prime candidates are never extinguished.

### Considerations

* **Survival Inequality**: For a "2" gap to survive, the creation of new "2" gaps (from adjacent 1-gaps, though rare for large $p$) and the initial count must exceed the destruction rate.
* **Deterministic Shrinkage**: Total cycle size $|R_k|$ follows Euler’s totient function $\phi(M_k)$.
* **Threshold Dynamics**: As $p$ increases, the "destruction probability" of a "2" gap decreases, favoring long-term persistence.

### Validation Methods

| Method | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Formal Verification** | Use `Stainless` to prove the survival invariant ($T_k > 0$) as a property of the `nextLevel` function. |
| **Diagnostic Counters** | Implement `countGapsOfSize(n)` to empirically track gap distribution across refinements. |
| **Threshold Testing** | Verify if $T_k > \lceil \frac{p}{p-2} \rceil$ holds across the first $N$ primes. |
